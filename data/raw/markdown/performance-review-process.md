# Performance Review Process

Document ID: HR-POL-009. Applies to all employees on the Nexora payroll who joined on or
before 31 December of the assessment year.

## 1. The year at a glance

The performance year runs **1 April to 31 March**, the same as the financial year and
deliberately different from the leave year, which is the calendar year.

| Stage | Window | Who acts | System |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goal setting | 1–30 April | Employee drafts, manager approves | PeopleHub → Performance |
| Continuous feedback | All year | Anyone | PeopleHub → Feedback |
| Mid-year check-in | 1–31 October | Manager and employee | PeopleHub |
| Self-assessment | 1–15 April | Employee | PeopleHub |
| Manager assessment | 16–30 April | Manager | PeopleHub |
| Normalisation | 1–20 May | Normalisation Council | Offline, recorded on PeopleHub |
| Rating release and conversation | 21 May – 5 June | Manager | Face to face, then PeopleHub |
| Compensation letters | 25 June | HR Operations | PeopleHub |
| Revision effective | 1 July | Payroll | — |

## 2. Goals

Between four and seven goals. Fewer than four and the picture is too coarse; more than
seven and nothing is a priority.


Every employee carries:

- at least one **delivery** goal — the work itself;
- at least one **learning** goal, aligned to the Individual Development Plan;
- at least one **values** goal, expressed as observable behaviour and not as an
  adjective; and
- for Band B4 and above, at least one **people** goal — mentoring, hiring, buddying,
  internal faculty, or the health of the team you lead.

Goals are written by the employee and approved by the manager. A goal that arrives fully
formed from the manager is a task list, and it will not survive contact with the year.

Goals may be revised at the mid-year check-in. Revising a goal because the business
changed is normal and expected.  Revising a goal in March because it was not met is not.

## 3. The rating scale

| Rating | Label | What it means | Indicative distribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Outstanding | Delivered beyond the role, visibly raised the standard for others | 5–8% |
| 4 | Exceeds Expectations | Consistently ahead of what the role requires | 20–25% |
| 3 | Meets Expectations | Did the job well. This is a good rating. | 55–60% |
| 2 | Partially Meets Expectations | Fell short on some material commitments | 8–12% |
| 1 | Below Expectations | Fell short across the role | 2–4% |

**On rating 3.** More than half of Nexora is rated 3 in any year, including many people
who had an excellent year. A 3 is not a warning, it is not a signal to look elsewhere,
and it is not what a manager gives when they cannot be bothered to argue for a 4.
Managers who describe a 3 apologetically are the single largest source of avoidable
attrition in the two months after rating release.

The distribution is a guide for the Normalisation Council, not a quota to be applied
inside one team. A manager may propose a distribution outside the guide with a written
justification, and the Council is required to consider it on the evidence.

## 4. Normalisation

The Normalisation Council for each function comprises the function head, all Band B5+
managers in the function,  and the HRBP, who facilitates and does not vote.

The Council exists to answer one question: **would this rating mean the same thing if it
had been given by a different manager in this function?** It is not there to force a
 curve, and it may not adjust a rating without a reason recorded against the individual.

A rating may be moved down only where the Council identifies specific evidence the
manager did not weigh. A manager whose rating is moved must be told before the employee
is,  and must be given the reason in terms they can defend in the conversation. A manager
delivering a rating they do not understand is the worst outcome the process can produce.

 ## 5. The conversation

Ratings are delivered face to face, or on video for remote colleagues, never first in
writing. The manager covers, in this order:

1. The rating, said plainly, in the first minute.
2. The evidence — specific work, specific behaviour, specific dates.
3. What would have to be different for the next band up.
4. The compensation outcome, when it is known.
5.  What the employee wants to say.

Do not open with fifteen minutes of context before saying the number. Everyone in the
room knows what the meeting is about and the delay is not kind.

## 6. Outcomes

| Rating | Increment guideline (FY 2026-27) | Variable pay multiplier | Promotion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 14%–18% | 1.50× | Eligible |
| 4 | 10%–13% | 1.20× | Eligible |
| 3 | 6%–9% | 1.00× | Eligible on a second consecutive 3 with a readiness assessment |
| 2 | 0%–4% | 0.50× | Not eligible |
| 1 | Nil | Nil | Not eligible; PIP applies |

Additional adjustments sit outside the guideline: market correction where compa-ratio is
below 0.85, critical-skill premium, pay equity correction, and the promotion increment.

## 7. Performance Improvement Plans

 A PIP follows a rating of 1, or a material decline during the year. It runs 60 days,
extendable once by 30.

A PIP must state: the specific gap, in observable terms; the standard to be met; the
support the Company will provide; the review dates; and the consequence of not meeting
the standard.  Fortnightly reviews are mandatory and recorded.  The HRBP attends the
opening and closing reviews.

A PIP that is really a managed exit is a misuse of the process, and it is visible to
everyone watching. If separation is the intent, say so and handle it as a separation.

## 8.  Promotion

Eligibility requires 18 months in the current band,  a rating of 4 or 5 (or two
consecutive 3s with a documented readiness assessment), an open position at the target
band in the approved manpower plan,  and — for B4 and above — clearance by a Promotion
Panel comprising the function head, a B6+ leader from another function, and the HRBP.

Promotion to B5 and above additionally requires completion of NEX-LDR-101 Leadership
Foundations.

Promotions are effective 1 July. Off-cycle promotions need CHRO approval and are capped
at 1% of function headcount per financial year.

## 9. Appeals

An employee who believes their rating was reached unfairly may appeal to the function
head within 15 working days of the rating conversation, and thereafter to the CHRO. The
appeal must identify what was not considered, or what was considered wrongly.
Disagreement with a fairly reached judgement is not, by itself, grounds for appeal.

Appeals are recorded and reported to the ELT in aggregate each cycle.

## 10. Common failures, and what we expect instead

| Failure | Expectation |
|---|---|
| Feedback saved up for March | Feedback within a week of the thing it concerns |
| "You know what you need to work on" | Name it, with an example and a date |
| Recency bias — the last six weeks decide the year | Notes kept through the year in PeopleHub → Feedback |
| A surprise rating | Nothing in the review that was not said earlier |
| Rating the person's potential | Rate the year that happened |
| Rating a long-tenured colleague on habit | Rate against the current role, every year |
