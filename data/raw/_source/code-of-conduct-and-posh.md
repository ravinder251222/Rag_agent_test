# PART A — CODE OF CONDUCT

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 A Message on Purpose

Nexora Technologies holds client source code, client production systems, and the personal
data of millions of end customers. Our clients grant us that access on the strength of a
judgment about our character, not only our capability. This Code exists to make that
judgment safe.

The Code cannot anticipate every situation. Where it is silent, apply the following test
before acting:

1. **Is it legal?** Under Indian law and the law of the jurisdiction concerned.
2. **Is it within policy?** Under this Code and the policies referenced in Clause 1.4.
3. **Is it honest?** Would the description you would give of your action match what you
   actually did?
4. **Would it survive publication?** Would you be comfortable if the action were reported
   accurately to your team, the client and your family?

If the answer to any of these is "no" or "I am not sure", stop and ask. The channels are in
Clause 12.

### 1.2 Who This Code Applies To

This Code applies to every director, officer and employee of Nexora Technologies Private
Limited, and to every probationer, Graduate Engineer Trainee, fixed-term contract employee
and intern. Contractors, consultants, vendors and staffing partners are bound by the
equivalent obligations in the Third Party Code of Conduct (LEG-POL-003), which every
purchase order and master services agreement incorporates by reference.

The Code applies wherever you are: at a Nexora office, at a client site, at a conference, at
an offsite, on a client call from home, and on any online forum where you are identifiable
as a Nexora employee.

### 1.3 Your Obligations Under This Code

Every person to whom this Code applies must:

(a) read the Code and record an electronic acknowledgement on PeopleHub on joining and
    annually thereafter, by 30 April each year;
(b) complete course NEX-HR-103 (Code of Conduct and Business Ethics) within thirty (30) days
    of joining and annually thereafter;
(c) complete the annual Conflict of Interest declaration on PeopleHub by 30 April;
(d) report a suspected breach of the Code promptly through any channel in Clause 12; and
(e) cooperate fully and honestly with any investigation, including by preserving records.

Managers at Band B4 and above carry the additional obligation to model the Code, to create
conditions in which team members can raise concerns without cost, and to escalate any
concern raised with them within two (2) working days.

## 2. Integrity in Business Dealings

### 2.1 Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption

Nexora prohibits bribery in every form, in every country, without exception and without a
materiality threshold. No employee may, directly or through a third party, offer, promise,
give, request, agree to receive or accept any financial or other advantage intended to
induce or reward the improper performance of a function.

This prohibition applies to dealings with government officials and to purely commercial
dealings. It extends to conduct that would be an offence under the Prevention of Corruption
Act, 1988, the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act of the United States, and the Bribery Act 2010
of the United Kingdom, each of which applies to some part of Nexora's client base.

**Facilitation payments** — small unofficial payments to speed up a routine government action
such as a visa, a licence or a customs clearance — are prohibited without exception, even
where they are customary in the location concerned. If you are asked for one, refuse, and
report it to ethics@nexoratech.in the same day. Where personal safety is at risk, protect
your safety first, pay if you must, and report immediately afterwards; you will not face
disciplinary action for a payment made under duress that is promptly reported.

### 2.2 Gifts and Hospitality

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Gift received, value up to INR 5,000 | Permitted; no declaration required |
| Gift received, value above INR 5,000 | Must be declared on the PeopleHub Gift Register within 7 days; retained only with function head approval, otherwise surrendered to HR for the annual charity draw |
| Gift of cash, cash equivalent, gift card, loan or share, any value | Prohibited; must be refused and reported |
| Gift to or from a government official, any value | Prohibited without prior written approval of the General Counsel |
| Meal or hospitality, value up to INR 8,000 per person | Permitted where business is discussed and the host is present |
| Hospitality above INR 8,000 per person, or any travel or accommodation paid by a third party | Requires prior written approval of the function head and the General Counsel |
| Gift or hospitality during an active tender or contract negotiation | Prohibited, at any value |

Gifts of nominal promotional value bearing a company logo — a notebook, a pen, a mug — are
outside the register.

### 2.3 Conflicts of Interest

A conflict of interest exists where your personal interest, or that of a relative, could
reasonably be seen to influence, or to have influenced, a decision you take on behalf of
Nexora. A perceived conflict damages trust as effectively as an actual one.

Situations that must be declared:

1. A financial interest above 2% of the equity, or above INR 5,00,000 in value, in a client,
   vendor, competitor or prospective vendor of Nexora.
2. A directorship, partnership, advisory role or trusteeship in any commercial entity.
3. A relative — as defined in Section 2(77) of the Companies Act, 2013 — employed by, or
   holding a material interest in, a client, vendor or competitor.
4. A relative employed by Nexora, whether or not in the same reporting line.
5. Participation in any selection, evaluation, procurement or appraisal decision affecting a
   relative, a close personal friend, or a person with whom you have or had a romantic
   relationship.
6. Any outside engagement, paid or unpaid, that uses Nexora time, assets, information or
   name.
7. A personal relationship with a person you supervise, directly or indirectly.

Declarations are made on PeopleHub → Compliance → Conflict of Interest, on joining, annually
by 30 April, and within seven (7) days of the circumstances arising. A declared conflict is
not itself a breach; failing to declare it is.

**Personal relationships in the reporting line.** A romantic or familial relationship between
an employee and a person in their reporting line, direct or indirect, must be declared to the
HRBP within seven days. Nexora does not prohibit such relationships, but it will restructure
the reporting line, the appraisal responsibility, or both. Neither party may participate in
any decision affecting the other's compensation, rating, promotion, staffing or discipline.

### 2.4 Outside Employment and Moonlighting

Employees may not accept concurrent employment, a retainer, a consulting engagement, or a
directorship with any other commercial entity without the prior written approval of the CHRO,
recorded on PeopleHub.

Permitted without approval:

- passive investment in listed securities, mutual funds and similar instruments, subject to
  Clause 2.6;
- unpaid teaching, mentoring, academic writing and conference speaking, provided it does not
  disclose Company or client information and does not occur during working hours;
- contribution to open-source projects that are unrelated to any client engagement you work
  on, using personal equipment and personal time, subject to ISMS-POL-004;
- income from writing, the arts, sport, agriculture and family property;
- unpaid office in a registered non-profit, resident welfare association or professional body.

Approval will normally be refused where the outside engagement is with a client, a vendor or
a competitor of Nexora; where it uses skills, tools or knowledge acquired in a client
engagement; or where it would occupy time during Nexora working hours.

### 2.5 Fair Competition

Nexora competes on the quality and price of its work. Employees must not:

(a) enter into or discuss any arrangement with a competitor concerning prices, bid rigging,
    market allocation, or a no-poach agreement, which would breach the Competition Act, 2002;
(b) obtain a competitor's confidential information by deception, or accept it from a person
    who is under an obligation not to disclose it, including a new joiner from a competitor;
(c) disparage a competitor with a statement that is untrue or that cannot be substantiated;
    or
(d) discuss Nexora's pricing, margins or bidding intentions at an industry association
    meeting or an informal gathering of competitors.

**New joiners.** A person joining Nexora from a competitor must not bring, disclose or use
any confidential information belonging to the former employer. Hiring managers must brief
every such joiner in writing on the first day and must record the briefing on PeopleHub.

### 2.6 Insider Trading

Nexora Technologies is an unlisted private company; its own shares are not publicly traded.
However, employees regularly receive unpublished price-sensitive information (UPSI) about
listed clients — an unannounced acquisition, an earnings figure, a system failure, a
regulatory action, a large contract.

Employees who possess UPSI about any listed entity must not deal in that entity's securities,
must not recommend that anyone else deal, and must not pass the information to any person who
does not need it for their work. This obligation continues until the information is
genuinely public. Breach is an offence under the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading)
Regulations, 2015, and exposes both the individual and Nexora to prosecution.

Employees on a client engagement designated "Restricted" by the General Counsel are placed on
a restricted list and must pre-clear all personal trades in that client's securities through
LEG-POL-007.

### 2.7 Accurate Records

Every record you create on behalf of Nexora — a timesheet, an expense claim, a status report,
a test result, a defect log, a code review sign-off, a certificate of completion, an audit
response — must be complete and accurate.

Specifically prohibited:

- recording hours not worked, or recording hours to a client or project code other than the
  one on which the work was performed;
- submitting an expense claim for an expense not incurred, or for a personal expense;
- signing off a test cycle, security review or code review that was not performed;
- backdating any document;
- creating or maintaining any undisclosed fund, account or asset.

Falsification of records is serious misconduct under HR-POL-001, Clause 15.3 and will lead to
termination in nearly every case.

## 3. Confidentiality, Data and Intellectual Property

### 3.1 Confidential Information

Confidential information includes anything not in the public domain that Nexora or a client
would not wish disclosed: source code, architecture, credentials, contracts, pricing, bid
material, client roadmaps, incident detail, unreleased product information, employee
personal data, and the fact of a client relationship where the client has not agreed to a
public reference.

Rules:

1. Access, use or disclose confidential information only for the purpose for which access was
   granted.
2. Do not discuss client matters in public places — airports, cafés, cabs, lifts, coworking
   spaces, or on a video call in a shared room without headphones.
3. Do not store client material on a personal device, a personal email account or a personal
   cloud service, and do not print it outside the office.
4. Do not use one client's material, code or approach for another client. Engagement teams
   working for competing clients are separated by information barriers documented in the
   engagement's Delivery Operating Model.
5. Confidentiality obligations survive the end of employment indefinitely.

### 3.2 Personal Data

Nexora processes personal data as a Data Fiduciary in respect of its own employees, and as a
Data Processor in respect of client end-customer data. The Digital Personal Data Protection
Act, 2023 governs both, alongside the client contract and, where applicable, the GDPR.

Every employee handling personal data must:

(a) process it only on documented instructions from the client or the Company;
(b) collect and access the minimum necessary for the purpose;
(c) never copy production personal data into a development, test or training environment
    without approved masking or synthetic substitution;
(d) never use production personal data to test a change, however small;
(e) report a suspected personal data breach to security-incident@nexoratech.in **within one
    (1) hour** of becoming aware of it — this timeline is contractual with several clients and
    supports the statutory notification obligation; and
(f) complete course NEX-LEG-104 annually.

### 3.3 Intellectual Property

All intellectual property created by an employee in the course of employment, or using
Company or client resources, vests in Nexora or, where the client contract so provides, in
the client. Employees assign such rights by their appointment letter and must execute any
further document reasonably required to perfect the assignment.

Employees must not incorporate third-party code, content, fonts, images or datasets into
Company or client deliverables without confirming the licence and recording it in the
engagement's Software Bill of Materials. Copyleft licences — including GPL-2.0, GPL-3.0,
AGPL-3.0 and SSPL — require the prior written approval of the Open Source Review Board before
any use in a deliverable.

### 3.4 Artificial Intelligence

Use of generative AI is permitted and encouraged within the boundaries of ISMS-POL-009:

1. Use only the enterprise instances provisioned by Nexora and listed on the NexServe
   software catalogue. Consumer accounts, personal API keys and unapproved browser extensions
   are prohibited for work purposes.
2. Never input client-confidential material, personal data, credentials, or proprietary
   source code into a service that is not on the approved list.
3. Where a client contract prohibits or restricts AI-assisted development, the restriction
   overrides this clause. The engagement's Delivery Operating Model records the position for
   every client.
4. AI-generated code must be reviewed by a human, tested, and scanned for licence and
   security issues before it is committed. The reviewer is accountable for the code as if
   they had written it.
5. Do not present AI-generated analysis, estimates or client deliverables as independently
   verified work unless you have verified it.

## 4. Respect at Work

### 4.1 Equal Opportunity and Non-Discrimination

Nexora makes every employment decision — hiring, staffing, appraisal, promotion,
compensation, training, discipline and separation — on merit. Discrimination on the basis of
religion, race, caste, sex, gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, place
of birth, descent, language, marital or parental status, pregnancy, disability, age, HIV
status, or any other status protected by law is prohibited.

Nexora provides reasonable accommodation to employees with disabilities in accordance with
the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016. Requests are made to the HRBP and are
determined within fifteen (15) working days.

### 4.2 Bullying, Harassment and Retaliation

Prohibited conduct includes:

- persistent criticism, humiliation or ridicule, in private or in front of others;
- shouting, abusive language, threats, or intimidating physical conduct;
- deliberate exclusion from meetings, information or opportunities;
- setting objectives that are designed to be unachievable;
- misuse of position to demand personal favours;
- offensive jokes or comments about a protected characteristic, whether or not directed at a
  specific person;
- circulating offensive material on Company systems or in team channels;
- retaliation of any kind against a person who raises a concern or participates in an
  inquiry.

Firm, fair and specific performance feedback delivered with respect is **not** bullying. A
manager is entitled — and expected — to set demanding standards, to say clearly when work is
not good enough, and to hold people to commitments.

### 4.3 Substance Use

Reporting for work, or being on Company or client premises, under the influence of alcohol or
any controlled substance is serious misconduct. Nexora offices are alcohol-free except at an
event expressly authorised in writing by the site head, at which responsible consumption is
expected and Company-arranged transport is provided.

An employee who voluntarily discloses a dependency before any incident occurs will be
supported through the Employee Assistance Programme and will not face disciplinary action for
the disclosure itself.

### 4.4 Workplace Safety

Every employee must comply with safety instructions, report hazards and near misses, and
participate in evacuation drills. Violence, threats of violence, and possession of a weapon
on Company premises are serious misconduct and will be reported to the police.

## 5. Company Assets and Communications

### 5.1 Use of Assets

Company assets — devices, network, software licences, meeting rooms, stationery, subscriptions
and time — are provided for Company work. Incidental personal use of the device and network is
permitted where it is reasonable in extent, does not interfere with work, does not incur
material cost, and does not involve prohibited content.

### 5.2 Social Media

Employees are free to use social media personally. When doing so:

1. Make clear that views are your own. Do not present a personal view as a Nexora position.
2. Never disclose client names, client work, unreleased information, incident detail,
   internal metrics, or photographs taken inside a delivery zone.
3. Do not post about a colleague in a manner that would breach Clause 4.2 if said at work.
4. Only the Marketing team and persons authorised by the CEO may speak for Nexora publicly or
   respond to a media enquiry. Forward all media enquiries to press@nexoratech.in.
5. Do not use the Nexora logo, name or brand assets in a personal capacity without approval.

### 5.3 Political Activity

Employees may participate in lawful political activity in their personal capacity, in their
own time, using their own resources. Nexora makes no political contributions of any kind and
no employee may make one on the Company's behalf or using Company funds. Do not use Company
premises, email, systems or the Nexora name for political activity.

### 5.4 Charitable Activity

Nexora supports employee volunteering through the Nexora Cares programme, which provides two
(2) days of paid volunteering leave per employee per financial year for work with a
registered non-profit. Solicitation of colleagues for donations is permitted only through the
Nexora Cares platform and never through direct or repeated personal approach.

## 6. Client and Vendor Relationships

### 6.1 Client Interactions

Represent status honestly. Do not report a milestone as complete when it is not, do not
conceal a defect, a delay or an incident, and do not commit to a scope, date or price without
the authority to do so. Where you have made an error that affects a client, escalate it to
your Delivery Manager the same day.

Do not accept an instruction from a client that would require you to breach this Code, Indian
law, or the client's own contractual restrictions. Escalate instead.

### 6.2 Client Property and Access

Access to a client's systems is granted for a defined scope and period. Employees must not
exceed the granted scope, must not share credentials, must not retain client data after the
engagement ends, and must surrender client-issued devices and passes on rotation off the
engagement. Access is revoked within twenty-four (24) hours of an employee's rotation off an
engagement or separation from the Company.

### 6.3 Vendors and Procurement

Procurement decisions are made on documented evaluation criteria by the Procurement function.
No employee may commit Nexora to a purchase, a licence or a contractual obligation without
authority under the Delegation of Authority matrix (FIN-POL-001). Splitting a purchase to
avoid an approval threshold is a breach of this Code.

## 7. Disciplinary Consequences

A breach of this Code is dealt with under HR-POL-001, Clause 15. Consequences range from
counselling to termination for cause, and may include recovery of loss, referral to law
enforcement, and reporting to a regulator or client where required by contract or law.

Factors considered are the seriousness of the breach, whether it was deliberate or negligent,
whether it was self-reported, the level of the individual, the harm caused, cooperation with
the investigation, and the disciplinary history.

Self-reporting a breach promptly is a substantial mitigating factor and is recorded as such.
Concealing a breach, or misleading an investigator, is a serious aggravating factor.

# PART B — PREVENTION OF SEXUAL HARASSMENT (POSH)

## 8. Statutory Basis and Commitment

This Part is issued under Section 19 of the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace
(Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 ("the POSH Act") and the rules made
thereunder. It is displayed at a conspicuous place at each Nexora workplace and on PeopleHub
→ Policies.

Nexora is committed to a workplace free of sexual harassment. Every complaint is taken
seriously, handled confidentially, and inquired into by a duly constituted Internal Committee
within the statutory timelines.

**Extension of protection.** The POSH Act confers rights on aggrieved women. Nexora extends
the identical complaint mechanism, the identical protections and the identical procedure to
every employee of any gender, and to persons of any gender identity, as a matter of Company
policy. Where a complaint is made by a person who is not an aggrieved woman under the Act,
the Internal Committee conducts the inquiry under this Policy using the same procedure, and
the outcome carries the same consequences, save that the statutory reporting obligations
apply only to complaints under the Act.

## 9. What Constitutes Sexual Harassment

### 9.1 Statutory Definition

Under Section 2(n) of the POSH Act, "sexual harassment" includes any one or more of the
following unwelcome acts or behaviour, whether directly or by implication:

(i) physical contact and advances;
(ii) a demand or request for sexual favours;
(iii) making sexually coloured remarks;
(iv) showing pornography;
(v) any other unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature.

### 9.2 Circumstances Amounting to Harassment

Under Section 3(2), the following circumstances, if they occur in relation to any act or
behaviour of sexual harassment, may amount to sexual harassment:

(a) implied or explicit promise of preferential treatment in employment;
(b) implied or explicit threat of detrimental treatment in employment;
(c) implied or explicit threat about present or future employment status;
(d) interference with work, or creation of an intimidating, offensive or hostile work
    environment;
(e) humiliating treatment likely to affect health or safety.

### 9.3 Illustrative Examples

The following, when unwelcome, are examples of conduct that may constitute sexual harassment
at Nexora. The list is illustrative and not exhaustive.

- Comments on a colleague's body, clothing, appearance or personal life of a sexual nature.
- Persistent requests for a date, a personal meeting or contact outside work after a refusal.
- Sending sexually explicit images, links, memes, emojis or messages on any channel,
  including a personal WhatsApp number obtained from the employee directory.
- Unnecessary physical contact — touching, hugging, patting, brushing past, blocking a path.
- Staring in a manner that causes discomfort; comments about a person's body on a video call.
- Sexual jokes, innuendo or "banter" in a team channel, a stand-up or an offsite.
- Displaying or circulating pornography on a Company device or network.
- Suggesting that a rating, a project allocation, an onsite opportunity or a promotion depends
  on a personal relationship.
- Spreading sexual rumours about a colleague.
- Repeated invitations to drink alcohol coupled with comments of a sexual nature.
- Recording or photographing a colleague without consent in circumstances of intimacy.

### 9.4 "Workplace" and "Employee"

"Workplace" for this Policy includes every Nexora office; any client site, vendor site or
third-party premises where an employee is present in the course of employment; any place
visited during the course of employment including transportation provided by the Company;
Company-organised events, offsites, training programmes and celebrations, including where
they involve alcohol; and any digital channel used for work, including email, Slack, Teams,
Zoom, the PeopleHub messaging module, and work-related groups on personal messaging
applications.

"Employee" includes permanent employees, probationers, trainees, interns, apprentices,
fixed-term contract employees, consultants, contractors, staffing-partner personnel and
volunteers, whether or not paid by Nexora, and whether the terms of engagement are express or
implied.

### 9.5 What Is Not Sexual Harassment

Legitimate and respectfully delivered performance feedback, a fair rating, a decision not to
promote, a change in project allocation, the enforcement of attendance rules, and ordinary
managerial supervision are not sexual harassment, even where they are unwelcome to the
recipient. Consensual conduct between adults is not harassment, though it may raise a
conflict of interest under Clause 2.3 and must be declared.

## 10. The Internal Committee

### 10.1 Constitution

An Internal Committee (IC) is constituted at each Nexora workplace under Section 4 of the
POSH Act. Each IC comprises:

(a) a **Presiding Officer**, who is a woman employed at a senior level at the workplace;
(b) not fewer than **two members** from among employees, preferably committed to the cause of
    women or having experience in social work or legal knowledge; and
(c) one **external member** from a non-governmental organisation or association committed to
    the cause of women, or a person familiar with issues relating to sexual harassment.

At least one half of the total members of each IC are women. Members hold office for a term
not exceeding three (3) years from the date of nomination.

### 10.5 Disqualification and Replacement

A member is removed from the IC where the member contravenes Section 16 (confidentiality),
is convicted of an offence or is subject to an ongoing inquiry, is found guilty in a
disciplinary proceeding, or has abused their position so as to render continuance
prejudicial to the public interest. A vacancy is filled within thirty (30) days.

A member who is a party to the complaint, who is in the reporting line of either party, or
who has a personal relationship with either party, recuses from that inquiry. The recusal is
recorded and a substitute member is nominated by the CHRO for that inquiry.

### 10.6 Powers of the Internal Committee

The IC has the powers of a civil court under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 in respect of
summoning and enforcing the attendance of any person and examining them on oath, and
requiring the discovery and production of documents (Section 11(3) of the POSH Act).

The Company will provide the IC with such facilities, assistance and access to records as it
requires, and will secure the attendance of any employee whom the IC wishes to examine.

## 11. Complaint and Inquiry Procedure

### 11.1 Who May Complain and How

A complaint may be made by the aggrieved person. Where the aggrieved person is unable to make
a complaint by reason of physical or mental incapacity or death, a complaint may be made by
the persons specified in Rule 6 of the POSH Rules, 2013, including a relative, a friend, a
co-worker, an officer of the National or State Commission for Women, or a person who has
knowledge of the incident with the written consent of the aggrieved person.

A complaint may be submitted:

- by email to **posh.ic@nexoratech.in**, which is accessible only to the Presiding Officers;
- in writing to the Presiding Officer for the site, in a sealed envelope;
- in person to any IC member; or
- through PeopleHub → Helpdesk → POSH Complaint, which routes directly to the IC and is not
  visible to HR Operations or the reporting manager.

Six (6) copies of the complaint, together with supporting documents and the names and
addresses of witnesses, are required by Rule 7(1). Where the complaint is received by email
or through PeopleHub, the IC secretary prepares the copies; the complainant is not required
to do so.

**Assistance in making a complaint.** Where a complainant is unable to reduce the complaint to
writing, any IC member will provide reasonable assistance in doing so, and will record that
assistance was provided.

### 11.2 Time Limit

A complaint must be made within **three (3) months** of the date of the incident, or, in the
case of a series of incidents, within three months of the date of the last incident.

The IC may extend this period by a further **three (3) months** where it is satisfied that
circumstances prevented the complainant from filing within the original period. The reasons
for an extension are recorded in writing.

### 11.3 Acknowledgement and Initial Steps

| Step | Timeline |
|---|---|
| Acknowledgement to the complainant | 2 working days of receipt |
| IC convened to consider the complaint | 5 working days of receipt |
| Copy of complaint provided to the respondent | 7 working days of receipt |
| Respondent's written reply with documents and witness list | 10 working days of receiving the complaint |
| Inquiry commenced | 15 working days of receipt |
| Inquiry completed | 90 days of receipt |
| Inquiry report submitted to the employer and to both parties | 10 days of completion of the inquiry |
| Action taken on the recommendations | 60 days of receipt of the report |
| Appeal, if any, to the appropriate court or tribunal | 90 days of the recommendations |

### 11.4 Conciliation

Before initiating an inquiry, the IC may, **at the request of the complainant and only at
that request**, take steps to settle the matter through conciliation (Section 10).

- No monetary settlement may be made the basis of a conciliation.
- Where a settlement is arrived at, the IC records it and forwards it to the employer, who
  acts on it. No further inquiry is conducted.
- Where the complainant informs the IC that a term of the settlement has not been complied
  with, the IC proceeds to an inquiry.
- Conciliation is never proposed by the IC, the employer or the respondent, and the
  complainant is under no obligation to consider it.

### 11.5 Interim Relief

At the written request of the complainant, and at any stage before the inquiry is completed,
the IC may recommend to the employer that it (Section 12):

(a) transfer the complainant or the respondent to another workplace or another team;
(b) grant the complainant leave of up to **three (3) months**, which is in addition to the
    leave the complainant is otherwise entitled to and is fully paid;
(c) restrain the respondent from reporting on the work performance of the complainant, or
    from writing their confidential report or appraisal, and assign that responsibility to
    another person; or
(d) grant such other relief as may be appropriate.

Nexora implements a recommendation for interim relief within five (5) working days.
Interim relief is protective and is not a finding against the respondent.

### 11.6 Conduct of the Inquiry

1. The inquiry is conducted in accordance with the principles of natural justice.
2. Both parties are given a fair opportunity to be heard, to present documents, to name
   witnesses, and to respond to the material relied upon.
3. Neither party may bring a legal practitioner to represent them before the IC
   (Rule 7(6)). A party may be accompanied by a colleague of their choice who is not a
   witness in the matter.
4. The parties are not permitted to cross-examine each other directly. Questions are put
   through the IC.
5. A minimum of three (3) members, including the Presiding Officer, must be present at every
   hearing (Section 11(3) proviso).
6. Where either party fails, without sufficient cause, to attend three consecutive hearings,
   the IC may, after giving fifteen (15) days' written notice, terminate the proceedings or
   give an ex parte decision.
7. All proceedings are recorded in minutes signed by the members present. Recordings by any
   party are prohibited.
8. The standard of proof is the balance of probabilities.

### 11.7 Findings and Recommendations

Where the IC concludes that the allegation is **not proved**, it recommends that no action be
taken and records its reasons. A finding that an allegation is not proved is not a finding
that the complaint was false or malicious.

Where the IC concludes that the allegation is **proved**, it recommends:

(a) action in accordance with this Policy and the service rules, which may include a written
    warning, a warning entered on the personnel record, withholding of promotion, withholding
    of an increment, withholding of variable pay, counselling, community service, suspension,
    or termination of service; and
(b) deduction from the respondent's salary or wages of such sum as it considers appropriate
    to be paid to the complainant or to their legal heirs, having regard to the mental trauma,
    pain, suffering and emotional distress caused; the medical expenses incurred; the loss of
    career opportunity; the income and financial status of the respondent; and the
    feasibility of payment in a lump sum or in instalments (Sections 13(3)(ii) and 15).

Where the respondent is not an employee — a contractor, a client employee, a vendor — the IC
recommends action available under the contract, which may include removal from the
engagement, revocation of site access, escalation to the respondent's employer, and referral
to the police.

### 11.8 Malicious Complaints

Where the IC concludes that a complaint was made maliciously, or that the complainant or a
witness knowingly produced a forged or misleading document, it may recommend action against
that person (Section 14).

The IC must record its reasons, and an inquiry into the malice must be conducted before any
recommendation is made. **The mere inability to substantiate a complaint, or the absence of
adequate proof, does not attract this clause.** Nexora will not treat an unproved complaint
as a false complaint, and no adverse entry is made against a complainant whose complaint is
not proved.

### 11.9 Confidentiality

The contents of the complaint, the identity and addresses of the complainant, the respondent
and the witnesses, any information relating to the conciliation and inquiry proceedings, and
the recommendations and action taken must not be published, communicated or made known to the
public, the press or the media in any manner (Section 16).

Breach of confidentiality by any person, including an IC member, attracts a penalty under
Section 17 and is serious misconduct under this Code. Information may be disclosed only to
the extent required to implement the recommendations, to comply with the law, or to respond
to a lawful order.

The IC maintains its records in a locked cabinet and an access-controlled repository
administered by the Presiding Officer, separate from PeopleHub and inaccessible to HR
Operations, the reporting managers or the ELT.

### 11.10 Prohibition of Retaliation

No person may victimise, intimidate or retaliate against a complainant, a witness, an IC
member, or any person who assists in an inquiry. Retaliation includes an adverse change to
rating, allocation, compensation, working conditions or reporting line, exclusion, or social
ostracism.

Retaliation is a distinct and serious misconduct, is inquired into separately, and attracts
consequences up to termination irrespective of the outcome of the underlying complaint.

### 11.11 Right to Criminal Proceedings

Nothing in this Policy prevents a complainant from initiating criminal proceedings. Where the
conduct complained of constitutes an offence under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, or any
other law, the IC informs the complainant of their right to initiate action, and, at the
complainant's request, assists in doing so. Where the complainant chooses to file a police
complaint, the internal inquiry continues unless the IC determines that it should be stayed.

## 12. Speaking Up and the Vigil Mechanism

### 12.1 Channels

| Channel | Contact | Anonymous? | Handled by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reporting manager or skip-level manager | Directly | No | Manager, escalated within 2 working days |
| HR Business Partner | Function HRBP | No | HR |
| Ethics mailbox | ethics@nexoratech.in | No | General Counsel |
| IntegriLine hotline | 1800-419-6272 | Yes, if chosen | Independent third party, reported to the Audit Committee |
| IntegriLine web portal | integriline.nexoratech.in | Yes, if chosen | Independent third party |
| POSH complaints | posh.ic@nexoratech.in | No (identity known to the IC only) | Internal Committee |
| Audit Committee direct access | audit.chair@nexoratech.in | No | Chair of the Audit Committee |

IntegriLine operates twenty-four hours a day in English, Hindi, Kannada, Marathi, Tamil,
Telugu and Bengali. A caller who chooses anonymity receives a case reference and a call-back
date, and may follow up without identifying themselves.

### 12.2 How Concerns Are Handled

| Step | Timeline |
|---|---|
| Acknowledgement of a concern | 3 working days |
| Triage and assignment of an investigator | 7 working days |
| Investigation completed | 45 days, extendable to 90 days for complex matters with written reasons |
| Outcome communicated to the reporter, to the extent permissible | 10 working days of conclusion |
| Report to the Audit Committee | Quarterly, in summary; immediately where the matter involves the ELT, fraud above INR 25,00,000, or a regulator |

An investigator must have no involvement in, and no reporting relationship to, the matter or
the persons concerned. Concerns involving a member of the ELT are investigated under the
direction of the Chair of the Audit Committee using external counsel.

### 12.3 Protection for Reporters

A person who raises a concern in good faith is protected against retaliation, even where the
concern turns out to be mistaken. Good faith means an honest belief on reasonable grounds; it
does not require that the concern be correct.

Protection does not extend to a person who knowingly makes a false report, or who reports in
order to deflect an inquiry into their own conduct. Such conduct is itself a breach of this
Code.

Each IC prepares an annual report for the calendar year containing the number of complaints
received, the number disposed of, the number pending for more than ninety days, the number of
workshops conducted, and the nature of the action taken. The report is submitted to the
employer and to the District Officer of the relevant district by 31 January.
